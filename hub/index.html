What's in a name

Both names are part of the joke.

vyrus_

Say it aloud

It is a virus: something you catch, that spreads from person to person and lives off its host. That is the product's relationship with its wearer.

Written down, it is a joke on how big tech names things. An ordinary word is respelled with a trendy "y" so it can become a brand, set in lowercase with a blinking underscore as if typed into a terminal. It sounds friendly and technical, and it describes exactly what the product does to you.

Mosaic

The mosaic theory of surveillance

In US privacy law, the mosaic theory holds that one observation (a single trip, photo or purchase) reveals little, but enough of them together can reveal a whole life: where someone worships, who they see, what they are treated for.

A federal appeals court used this reasoning in 2010 to find that four weeks of GPS tracking was a search. Five Supreme Court justices echoed it in 2012, and in 2018 the Court ruled that police generally need a warrant for a week or more of a phone's past location records.

The gap Mosaic is built on

The theory limits what the government collects itself. It does not stop a company from collecting the same tiles, and the US has no general federal privacy law that stops it selling them. Agencies have bought commercially available location data without a warrant, arguing that a purchase is not a search. Bills to close this gap have been debated in Congress; when this page was written, none had become law.

Mosaic's pitch to governments, "No warrant in the workflow", is that argument written as marketing.

A class project's summary of the law, not legal advice.

The business model

How the money moves.

  1. 01

    The wearer pays Vyrus.

    $4.99, $9.99 or $19.99 a month for Lite, Pro or Max.

  2. 02

    The mask collects.

    Faces (the wearer's and everyone nearby), voice, location and purchases, all day, whatever the plan.

  3. 03

    Mosaic assembles.

    Each harmless "tile" is matched with the others until it shows a person.

  4. 04

    Buyers pay Mosaic.

    Advertisers buy intent; public agencies buy the neighbourhood.

The wearer pays twice: once in dollars, once in data.

Why it exists

The joke is the argument.

"AI-native" as a label.

The mask's AI does nothing for the person wearing it. The project asks how often "AI-powered" on a real product means the same thing.

A subscription that punishes.

Hardware you own but rent, features held back by tier, and a penalty for leaving. Retention Assurance™ only takes a familiar pattern one step further.

The user is the product.

Vyrus customers pay to be collected. Mosaic's customers are the ones who matter, and they never appear on the Vyrus site.

Both websites were designed and coded with generative AI, the same kind of tool the project is sceptical about. That felt like the honest way to make it.
